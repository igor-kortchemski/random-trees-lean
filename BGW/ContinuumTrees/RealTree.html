    Real trees #

    A metric space is a real tree if any two points s, t are joined by a geodesic segment, this segment is unique, and every injective continuous path from s to t has the same image as the segment. This is the standard definition.

    We prove that a complete metric space satisfying the four-point condition (0-hyperbolicity) and having midpoints is a real tree:

    • midpoints and completeness give geodesic segments (dyadic construction);
    • the four-point condition forces their uniqueness, and characterizes their points as those c with d(s,c) + d(c,t) = d(s,t);
    • removing an interior point of the segment separates s from t (via the Gromov product at that point), so every path from s to t passes through every point of the segment, and injectivity forbids any other point.

    The Gromov product and its nonnegativity are reused from Mathlib.
