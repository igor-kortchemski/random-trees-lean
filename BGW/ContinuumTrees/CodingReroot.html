    The continuous excursion obtained by changing the root #

    Circular time shift uses the representative in [0, 1). This time map is measurable, but is neither continuous nor surjective on the closed unit interval in general. Composing with the coding projection closes the endpoints and gives a continuous surjection onto the tree. Distance from the point represented by the chosen root time then gives a nonnegative continuous coding function with zero endpoints.

    This is the construction in Le Gall, Random trees and applications, Lemma 2.3, pp. 273–274, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0511515v1. The exact distance identity is proved from attained interval minima, treating both sides of the circular cut and intervals crossing it. It descends to a surjective isometry sending the new root to the chosen vertex. Circular time shift preserves Lebesgue probability, by translation invariance of Haar measure on the unit additive circle. Consequently this same isometry preserves the actual mass measure.
