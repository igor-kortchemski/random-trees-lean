    The first-passage convolution for forests #

    For an offspring probability measure μ, 1 ≤ ℓ and every N, decomposing the walk of independent offspring counts at its first passage time q to -ℓ,

    P(S_N = -ℓ) = ∑_{q ≤ N} P(|𝒢_ℓ| = q) P(S_{N-q} = 0)

    (pi_countForestBridges_eq_sum), where P(S_n = -ℓ) is the mass of countForestBridges ℓ n under Measure.pi (fun _ : Fin n => μ) and P(|𝒢_ℓ| = q) is forestWeight (fun d => μ {d}) ℓ q. The identity is unconditional: no criticality, positivity or lattice hypothesis, and it holds for N < ℓ as well, where both sides vanish. For ℓ = 1 the factor P(|𝒢_1| = q) is the mass of sizeEvent q under the Bienaymé–Galton–Watson law (pi_countBridges_eq_sum). It is obtained from the word-level decomposition tsum_forestBridgeWords_eq_sum by the reindexing of ForestWalkLaw. The uniform cyclic-shift representation and the conditional bridge law are not part of this module. Adapted from PoissonDeclumping/Bridge/Kemperman.lean (walkMass_neg_eq_sum).

    theorem BGW.countForestBridges_zero (n : ℕ) :
    countForestBridges 0 n = {ω : Fin n → ℕ | ∑ i : Fin n, ω i = n}

    The walk ends at 0 exactly when the counts sum to n.
