    Normalization for offspring laws of mean at most one #

    If the offspring law μ has mean ∫⁻ k, k ∂μ ≤ 1 and μ {1} ≠ 1, then 1 is the only fixed point of the generating function in [0, 1] (eq_one_of_offspringGF_eq). Since the probability of finiteness is such a fixed point (isLeast_bienaymeLaw_finiteVertices), it equals one: the tree is almost surely finite, and the weights of the finite plane trees sum to one. The hypothesis μ {1} ≠ 1 is necessary: for μ = dirac 1 the mean is one, f s = s for all s, and the probability of finiteness is zero. The mean is the expectation ∫⁻ k, k ∂μ in [0, ∞]; laws of infinite mean and the supercritical classification are not treated here. Adapted from PoissonDeclumping/Bienayme/Normalization.lean, where the mean is exactly one.
