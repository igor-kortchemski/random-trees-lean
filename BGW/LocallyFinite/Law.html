    The Bienaymé–Galton–Watson law on locally finite trees #

    For an arbitrary probability measure μ on ℕ, offspringFieldLaw μ makes the potential offspring counts at all addresses independent with law μ, and bienaymeLaw μ is the law of the pruned tree. Nothing is assumed about the mean or support of μ; the tree may be infinite. This is the general part of Freezing1/PlaneBienaymeLaw.lean, without its geometric specialization.

    The branching property, uniqueness and the law of the finite trees are in Branching.lean, Unique.lean and Atoms.lean.

    Independent potential offspring counts, one for each address.

    The probability hypothesis is not used by the formula, but it fixes the domain of the definition: Measure.infinitePi is the zero measure unless every component is a probability measure, so without it the definition would expose that default value instead of an offspring law.
