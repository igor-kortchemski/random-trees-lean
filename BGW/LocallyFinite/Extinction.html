    Extinction by a finite depth and the least fixed point #

    extinctBy n is the event that every present address has length strictly less than n: the root has depth zero, so extinctBy 0 = ∅ and extinctBy 1 = {leaf}, and extinctBy n is extinction by generation n. Under bienaymeLaw μ, for every offspring probability measure μ (no mean, nondegeneracy or support condition), the root decomposition and the branching property give P(extinctBy (n + 1)) = f (P(extinctBy n)) with f = offspringGF μ, hence P(extinctBy n) = f^[n] 0. A tree is finite exactly when it lies in some extinctBy n, so the probability q of finiteness is the supremum of these iterates, satisfies f q = q, and is the smallest fixed point of f in [0, 1]. By bienaymeLaw_finiteVertices, it is also the total weight of the finite plane trees. The fixed-point argument follows PoissonDeclumping/Bienayme/Normalization.lean (offspringGF_bienayme_univ) and the height recursion of PoissonDeclumping/Bienayme/Basic.lean, whose convention is shifted by one here. Classification by the mean, generation sizes and conditioning are not part of this module.
