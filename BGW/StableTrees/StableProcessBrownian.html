    Index-two stable processes and Donsker's theorem #

    At stability index two, the stable process law is the law of √2 times Brownian motion. The finite-dimensional increment characterization identifies the two laws. The stable-domain functional limit theorem then gives Donsker's theorem for critical offspring laws with finite variance and the normalization used throughout the project (P. Billingsley, Convergence of Probability Measures, 2nd ed., Wiley, 1999, Theorem 8.2; J. Bertoin, Lévy Processes, Cambridge University Press, 1996, Chapter VIII).

    source
    theorem BGW.StableTrees.stableProcessLaw_two {T : ℝ} (hT : 0 < T) :
    stableProcessLaw 2 T = ContinuumTrees.BrownianD.wienerD (NNReal.sqrt 2) T

    At θ = 2 the stable process law is the law of √2 · B.

    Filter.Tendsto (fun (n : ℕ) => stepPathLaw (realOffspringStepLaw μ) T (fun (n : ℕ) => √(∫ (z : ℤ), ↑z ^ 2 ∂stepLaw μ) / √2 * √↑n) n) Filter.atTop (nhds (ContinuumTrees.BrownianD.wienerD (NNReal.sqrt 2) T))

    Donsker's theorem for critical finite-variance offspring laws: with σ² = E[(ξ - 1)²], the step paths normalized by σ √n / √2 converge to wienerD (√2) T.
