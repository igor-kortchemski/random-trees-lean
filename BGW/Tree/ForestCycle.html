    Forest codes #

    For ℓ ≥ 1, a forest code is an integer word with all entries at least -1, sum -ℓ, and all proper prefix sums (the empty prefix included) strictly above -ℓ: the first passage to -ℓ happens at the terminal time. Prepending the artificial-root increment ℓ - 1 turns such a word into a tree excursion, so forest codes are exactly the increments of the ordered forests with ℓ components, each forest being determined by its code. The weight of a forest, the product of the weights of its components, is the walk weight of its code.

    The rotation of a word of sum -ℓ is a forest code exactly when its starting index is a strict running minimum of the prefix sums that later prefix sums do not undercut by ℓ or more. The generalized cycle lemma follows: a word of length n, entries at least -1 and sum -ℓ has exactly ℓ good rotation indices (indices are counted, not distinct rotated words, so periodic words are covered). Counting the pairs of a bridge word and a good index in two ways gives Kemperman's formula for forests in weight form, n * forestWeight p ℓ n = ℓ * (total walk weight of the words of length n and sum -ℓ), for arbitrary step masses p. Adapted from PoissonDeclumping/Bridge/Kemperman.lean (card_filter_rotate_firstPassageAt, forestMap_bijective, natCast_mul_forestSizeWeight); the forest coding goes through the artificial root and the tree excursion bijection instead of a second parser, and the first-passage convolution is not part of this module.
