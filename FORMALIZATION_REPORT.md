# Formalization report

The [coverage index](CLASSICAL_THEORY_COVERAGE.md) maps the classical theory of random-tree
scaling limits to the Lean declarations. This report records the discrete foundations,
source correspondence, and mathematical qualifications.

## Scope and environment

- **Paper:** Igor Kortchemski and Leonard Vetter, [*Rare subtree patterns in size-conditioned Bienaymé trees: Poisson approximation and declumping*, arXiv:2607.22291v1][P9]. Source line numbers below refer to the locally reviewed TeX file `poisson-declumping-arxiv.tex`; its SHA-256 at inventory time is `3b75c02ec5e8a7e12d32cccddfe463ae79ca596d1b8b239bc0352110b6472595`. Manuscript sources are not redistributed in this repository.
- **Library scope:** the reusable foundations described below and the results in the [C1–C39 coverage index](CLASSICAL_THEORY_COVERAGE.md). Paper-specific results belong to the companion projects.
- **Pinned environment:** Lean `leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0` in [lean-toolchain](lean-toolchain); Mathlib v4.35.0 at commit `56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679` in [lakefile.toml](lakefile.toml).

The reusable BGW foundations now include the generation process conditional on its entire
finite history, exponential tilting, geometric/uniform plane trees, discrete contours, fringe
blocks and the exact uniform-vertex fringe identity. The unrooted GHP quotient is a Polish
metric space with compactness and tightness transfer to GH. The deterministic real-tree
criterion and the scalar stable toolkit are integrated; their limits are described below.
P9 `cor:cyclicshift` and `lem:forest_estimates` are **Proved in atomic form**; the other 33 labeled P9 results remain
**Unproved in this library**. This does not describe their status in companion projects.
A completed entry needs a proved export or checked specialization, source correspondence,
and relevant build and axiom evidence. A conditional helper does not close a source result.

The P9 probability results use critical offspring with $\mu_1\ne1$, stable index $\alpha\in(1,2]$, and conditioning through admissible sizes. These are not assumptions of the deterministic library. Preserve positive-probability conditions, lattice endpoints and each statement's additional assumptions. The source defines microscopicity `(M)` at 215–225, structural condition `(S)` at 239–257, and pair non-clustering `(C)` at 275–295; `(S)` is a sufficient route to `(M)`, not an additional universal premise.

## Implemented foundations

All exports below are available through [BGW.lean](BGW.lean). Finite-tree operations belong to `BGW.PlaneTree`, the new carrier to `BGW.LocallyFinite`, forest operations to `BGW.PlaneForest`, and `IsTreeExcursion` to `BGW`.

- [Tree.lean](BGW/Tree.lean) defines `PlaneTree` as `WType (fun n : ℕ => Fin n)` and gives an exact list-of-children interface (`children_node`, `node_children`). `induction_on` and `casesOn` are the default `induction` and `cases` principles, each with a single `node ts` case. `fold` is bottom-up recursion over children, with `fold_node`; `size`, `vertices` and `degrees` are defined through it and remain evaluable by `decide`. The `WType` encoding is not used outside this file. The module also gives positive vertex size, re-rooted `fringe?` lookup, and explicit vertex and leaf predicates. Encodability, hence countability, is transferred from Mathlib's `WType` instance. No offspring-law assumptions enter these definitions.
- [Vertices.lean](BGW/Tree/Vertices.lean) enumerates the zero-based addresses in DFS order. `mem_vertices_iff`, `length_vertices` and `nodup_vertices` prove that it lists exactly the vertices, once each, including distinct occurrences of equal subtrees. Depth is address length, the root is `[]`, and `generationSize_zero` verifies the root generation. Prefix ancestry includes equality; the parent of a nonempty address is its `dropLast`.
- [Fringe.lean](BGW/Tree/Fringe.lean) proves composition of fringe lookup, prefix closure and gapless child indices at every vertex. It proves replacement, cut/restoration and size identities, including `size_graft_of_isLeaf`: the inserted tree's root is identified with the original leaf. Replacement at an invalid address is proved to leave the tree unchanged. These are deterministic prerequisites, **not** P9's probabilistic `lem:cut_graft`.

- [Tree/WalkWeight.lean](BGW/Tree/WalkWeight.lean) defines `walkWeight p xs = ∏ p (x + 1)` for a word `xs : List ℤ` of steps and a function `p : ℕ → ℝ≥0∞`; an entry below $-1$ would silently contribute `p 0`, but such words never occur because tree excursions have all entries at least $-1$. `PlaneTree.walkWeight_increments` proves `walkWeight p t.increments = weight p t`, since `increments` is `degrees` shifted by one and `(d - 1 + 1).toNat = d`. `PlaneTree.tsum_weight_size_eq_tsum_excursions` reindexes the sum over trees of size $n$ along the injective map `increments`: its image on trees of size $n$ is exactly the set of `IsTreeExcursion` words of length $n$ (`isTreeExcursion_iff_existsUnique`, `length_increments`), and the indicator form avoids a decidability instance for `IsTreeExcursion`. - [Tree/CycleLemma.lean](BGW/Tree/CycleLemma.lean) defines `bridgeWords n`, the words of length $n$ with entries at least $-1$ and sum $-1$. With $S_j$ the prefix sums of such a word $x$, the rotation by $i<n$ is a tree excursion exactly when $S_j\ge S_i+1$ for $j<i$ and $S_j\ge S_i$ for $i\le j<n$, that is when $i$ is the first index in $[0,n)$ at which $S$ is minimal (private lemma `isTreeExcursion_rotate_iff`, from a computation of the prefix sums of a rotation, `sum_take_rotate`). This gives the cycle lemma `exists_unique_rotate_isTreeExcursion`: existence by the first minimum and uniqueness of the *index* because two such indices would contradict each other's strict and weak inequalities (no periodicity argument is needed). The map `PlaneTree.shiftMap` sends a tree of size $n$ and an index $i<n$ to the rotation of its increments by $i$; it is injective (the complementary index $(n-i)\bmod n$ is the unique excursion rotation of the image) and surjective, and `PlaneTree.shiftEquiv` is the resulting equivalence. `walkWeight_rotate` holds since rotation permutes the entries. `PlaneTree.natCast_mul_tsum_weight_size` proves $n\sum_{|t|=n}\mathrm{weight}(p,t)=\sum_x\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{bridgeWords}\,n}(x)\,\mathrm{walkWeight}(p,x)$ by reindexing along `shiftEquiv`, with no hypothesis on `p`. The random-walk law, `P(S_n = -1)` and the forest version are not defined or claimed here.
- [LocallyFinite/WalkLaw.lean](BGW/LocallyFinite/WalkLaw.lean) reads the weighted Kemperman–Dwass identity as a statement about a random walk. `countBridges n = {ω : Fin n → ℕ | ∑ i, ω i + 1 = n}` is the event $S_n=-1$ for the walk with steps $\omega_i-1$ of independent offspring counts (a natural-number sum with $+1$, so that no truncated subtraction occurs and $n=0$ gives the empty event); `stepWord ω = List.ofFn (fun i => (ω i : ℤ) - 1)` is injective, lands in `bridgeWords n` exactly on `countBridges n`, has walk weight $\prod_i p(\omega_i)$, and every bridge word is a step word (`exists_stepWord_eq`, inverse by `toNat`). Reindexing along `stepWord` gives `tsum_bridgeWords_walkWeight`, and `pi_countBridges` expresses `Measure.pi (fun _ : Fin n => μ) (countBridges n)` as the sum of its point masses $\prod_i\mu(\{\omega_i\})$ (countable set, `tsum_indicator_apply_singleton`, `Measure.pi_singleton`). The main theorem `natCast_mul_bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent_eq_pi` is $n\,P(|T|=n)=P(S_n=-1)$ for every offspring probability measure. The walk is the product measure on counts rather than a measure on `ℤ` or a convolution, and only its endpoint is considered; forests, the uniform shift representation and the conditional bridge law are not part of this module.
- [Tree/ForestCycle.lean](BGW/Tree/ForestCycle.lean) defines `IsForestCode ℓ xs` (steps at least $-1$, sum $-\ell$, every proper prefix sum, the empty prefix included, strictly above $-\ell$: first passage to $-\ell$ at the terminal time). `isForestCode_iff_isTreeExcursion_cons` shows that for $\ell\ge1$ prepending the artificial-root increment $\ell-1$ turns it into a tree excursion, and `PlaneForest.isForestCode_iff_existsUnique` that forest codes are exactly the codes of the unique ordered forests with $\ell$ components, read off from the children of the decoded tree (via `increments_node` and `node_inj`); `PlaneForest.increments_injective` and `PlaneForest.walkWeight_increments` (product of component weights, without the root factor) complete the coding, with no second parser. - [LocallyFinite/ForestWalkLaw.lean](BGW/LocallyFinite/ForestWalkLaw.lean) reads the forest identity as a statement about the walk of offspring counts. `countForestBridges ℓ n = {ω | ∑ i, ω i + ℓ = n}` is the event $S_n=-\ell$; `stepWord` (from `WalkLaw.lean`, whose entry-only existence lemma was generalized to `exists_stepWord_eq_of_entries`) identifies the words of `forestBridgeWords ℓ n` with these vectors, `tsum_forestBridgeWords_walkWeight` reindexes, and `pi_eq_tsum_indicator` gives the mass of any set of count vectors under `Measure.pi (fun _ => μ)` as the sum of its point masses. - [LocallyFinite/SizeConditioning.lean](BGW/LocallyFinite/SizeConditioning.lean) defines `sizeEvent n = {T | FiniteVertices T ∧ {u | Present T u}.ncard = n}`, the event that the tree is finite with exactly $n$ vertices, leaves and root included. The finiteness conjunct is essential at $n=0$: an infinite set has `ncard` zero, so without it `sizeEvent 0` would be exactly the infinite trees (for $n\ge1$ it is implied). `ncard_present_toLocallyFinite` proves that the image of a plane tree has `t.size` present addresses (the positive addresses of its vertices, counted without repetition by `nodup_vertices`), hence `toLocallyFinite_mem_sizeEvent_iff` and `sizeEvent_eq_image`; the event is measurable as a countable image, and `sizeEvent_zero : sizeEvent 0 = ∅` follows from `size_pos`. `bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent` gives its mass as the sum of the weights of the plane trees of size $n$ through the restriction formula of `Atoms.lean`, and `bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent_ne_zero_iff` characterizes admissible sizes. For the conditioned law `(bienaymeLaw μ)[|sizeEvent n]` (Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.cond`), `natCast_mul_bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent` gives the weighted Kemperman–Dwass identity $n\,P(|T|=n)=\sum_{x\ \text{bridge}}\mathrm{walkWeight}(x)$ (`Tree/CycleLemma.lean`), `bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent_eq_tsum_excursions` and `bienaymeLaw_sizeEvent_ne_zero_iff_excursion` restate the mass and the admissibility through tree excursions (`Tree/WalkWeight.lean`), `isProbabilityMeasure_cond_sizeEvent` holds at an admissible size, `cond_sizeEvent_self` says it gives mass one to the size event, `cond_sizeEvent_eq_zero` records that it is the zero measure otherwise, and `cond_sizeEvent_singleton` gives the atom at `t.toLocallyFinite`. The source correspondence is P9's rooted ordered tree/fringe convention (189–190, 682–695), its walk coding (716–728), and its cut/graft convention (2497–2503). Zero-based child addresses are an implementation choice: the explicit one-based numbering in P9 is its DFS enumeration $u_1,\ldots,u_n$. The address membership, cardinality and vertex-degree bridges above establish this implementation's intended interpretation. The deterministic inverse coding is proved by a direct parser and a suffix-sum argument; this does not establish the paper's random-walk distribution identities. Fringe-block/cyclic decoding, the excursion law, generation-size transitions and the scalar LLT are now proved in their respective modules; the LLT requires `StableDomain`. The [coverage index](CLASSICAL_THEORY_COVERAGE.md) records the current scope.

to the existing finite-tree observable, measurable sizes, root-conditioned iid child sizes,

## Correspondence and outstanding checks

The labeled results completed here are `cor:cyclicshift` and `lem:forest_estimates`, in atomic form. The remaining labeled results are still consumer obligations. In particular, the second Poisson theorem states divergence of the declumped count, not a law of large numbers normalized by $c_Bn\pi_n$. The established `(M)+(C)` route points directly to `thm:PoissonApproximation(ii)`. Check and record that application when formalizing; the conclusion is not currently certified.

## Statement corrections

- **(a) Skorokhod representation.** The version on $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure is false for general separable metric spaces (refuted by a Bernstein set). The [0,1] version is delivered for Polish spaces only; the general separable version on some probability space (Dudley 1968) is now delivered (`exists_probabilitySpace_tendsto_ae_of_tendsto`).
- **(b) Tightness criteria.** For $\delta\ge T$ the modulus $w'(\delta)$ is $0$ by convention, so the criteria were vacuous. They are stated with $\delta<T$.
- **(c) Billingsley (12.17).** The bound $d\le e^{d^\circ}-1$ holds on $[0,1]$ only. On $[0,T]$ the delivered bound is $d\le \max(1,T)(e^{d^\circ}-1)$.
- **(d) Vervaat endpoint.** Vervaat's fact 3 requires the first infimum time $g>0$.
- **(e) Fixed jump times.** Countability of the fixed jump times of a law on $D$ needs $E$ separable.
- **(f) Aldous criterion.** An `_initial` variant with tightness at the initial time only was false and was replaced by fixed-time tightness (`isTightMeasureSet_of_aldous_fixedTimes`); counterexample $X^n_t=n\,\mathbf 1_{\{U\le t\}}$ with $U$ uniform.
- **(g) First-passage extraction.** The extraction result uses the hypothesis `passageZero`.
- **(h) Forest coding.** `tailMass_eq_forestTail` assumes $\mu(1)\ne1$.
- **(i) Gap-freeness of the height approximation.** `ae_support_gapfree_fixed` gives the
  fixed-time form used in the height construction.
- **(j) Height approximation.** The sequence `heightEps` is universal for the stable law,
  excursion measure, and unit excursion law. `heightUniv` is the limsup along that sequence.
- **(k) Excursion height.** The excursion height is reconstructed using agreement with
  `heightUniv` at Lebesgue-almost every age of the excursion.
- **(l) Forest contour.** The forest theorem uses the padded contour clock. The unpadded
  comparison is available separately in `BGW.StableTrees.UnpaddedContour`.
- **(m) Joint forest convergence.** C27 has both a product-of-paths and a vector-path form.
  The `_of_attracted` exports admit arbitrary admissible normings.
